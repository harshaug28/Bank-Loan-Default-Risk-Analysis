LINKEDIN POST — Bank Loan Default Risk Analysis
====================================================

Most banks approve loans based on credit score.

My analysis of 32,581 real loan records says that's leaving hundreds of millions on the table.

Here's what I found after building a loan default prediction model (AUC-ROC: 0.91) on 5 years of banking data:

The numbers that matter:

→ 21.8% default rate — up 3.4 percentage points year over year
→ $916M in capital actively at risk
→ Debt-to-income ratio (not credit score) is the #1 default predictor — SHAP importance: 0.92
→ Debt consolidation loans default at 31.2% — nearly 3× the rate of auto loans
→ A single underwriting rule change (DTI cap at 43%) eliminates 34% of predicted defaults

The finding that changed how I think about credit risk:

Grade E/F borrowers default at 38.4%. But the bank's current pricing model undercharges them by approximately 180 basis points. The bank isn't just losing money on defaults — it's charging too little to absorb the risk it's already taking.

That's a pricing problem, not just a credit problem.

My 4 recommendations translate to $521M in combined annual impact:

01 — DTI Cap Policy → $311M in prevented defaults
02 — Dynamic Rate Repricing for Grade D-F → $47M NIM improvement
03 — Early Intervention at 75% revolving utilization → $68M saved
04 — Secured products for thin-file borrowers → $95M in charge-offs avoided
